Cis-regulatory elements (CREs) encode when and where genes are expressed through
combinations of transcription-factor binding sites embedded in local sequence
context~\citep{Wasserman2004,ENCODE2012,Thurman2012,Roadmap2015}. Massively parallel
reporter assays (MPRAs) and STARR-seq now measure the regulatory activity of hundreds
of thousands of sequences in parallel~\citep{Melnikov2012,Arnold2013,Tewhey2016,
Inoue2017,Gordon2020}, enabling sequence-to-activity deep networks that recover
regulatory features directly from DNA and support computational design of synthetic
enhancers~\citep{Alipanahi2015,Kelley2016,Kelley2018,Avsec2021,Almeida2022,
Taskiran2024,Gosai2024}.

Most regulatory-design studies have emphasized de novo generation or score
maximization~\citep{Vaishnav2022,Almeida2024,Gosai2024}. A complementary and biologically
grounded strategy is \emph{minimal editing}: retuning an existing natural CRE for
greater cell-type selectivity while leaving nearly all of its sequence unchanged. The
natural parent provides a validated scaffold, and the substitution budget gives a
direct measure of design distance. The same constraint, however, makes the search
problem demanding: a small number of edits must improve the target-versus-off-target
activity margin without creating implausible sequence patterns or overfitting to the
model directing the search.

This challenge is central to the practical use of deep networks for regulatory-sequence
design. A highly optimized sequence can move into a region of sequence space where the
directing predictor is confident for the wrong reason, even when held-out predictive
performance is strong~\citep{Whalen2022,deBoer2024,Ovadia2019,Rafi2025}. Standard
greedy search compounds this risk because each substitution is retained solely for its
immediate score gain under a single objective. Beam search explores a broader set of edit
paths, but a wider search under the same predictor does not by itself establish that
the selected signal transfers across models. Cross-model generalization---rather than
single-model optimization---is therefore the key requirement for converting
model-guided search into experimentally actionable candidate sequences.

We developed SafeEdit-CRE to address this problem (Fig.~\ref{fig:workflow}). The
published Malinois predictor~\citep{Gosai2024} provides the primary cell-type
specificity objective. An architecture-diverse residual-dilated CNN ensemble serves as
an independent evaluation reviewer during constrained beam search, while
validation-calibrated uncertainty and sequence-domain controls (strand consistency,
GC shift, homopolymers, 6-mer naturalness) guard against out-of-distribution edits.
Selected sequences are then assessed by a second independently trained multi-kernel
ensemble that is held out from the search process, providing an additional layer of
cross-model evaluation. Fixed edit budgets of 1, 5, 10, and 20 nucleotides allow the
effect of design freedom to be quantified without conflating minimal editing with
unconstrained sequence generation.

We benchmark SafeEdit-CRE against three comparators: random substitutions as a null
baseline, iterative greedy optimization to test whether local score maximization is
sufficient, and a compute-matched primary-model beam that uses the same beam width and
expansion schedule as SafeEdit but only the primary predictor score. The compute-matched
beam is critical because it isolates the benefit of cross-model selection from the
benefit of a larger candidate pool. We evaluate four complementary endpoints: cross-model
generalization on 600 held-out natural CRE parents across K562, HepG2, and SK-N-SH; a
nine-criterion design-quality benchmark on 96 independent parents; stratified analyses
by cell type and edit budget; and complete re-search ablations that identify which
components govern the trade-off between predicted selectivity and uncertainty.

Our results demonstrate three main findings. First, relative to greedy search,
SafeEdit-CRE substantially improves cross-model cell-type-specificity margin. Second,
relative to a compute-matched beam search, SafeEdit-CRE preserves equivalent predicted
specificity while selecting lower-uncertainty variants. Third, SafeEdit-CRE raises
design-criteria pass rates from 38.5\% to 61.5\% and yields 78 high-priority candidate
sequences spanning all three cell types and four budgets. These findings establish
constrained cross-model minimal editing as a practical strategy for producing compact,
experimentally actionable CRE variant libraries from natural sequence scaffolds.
