\begin{abstract}
Sequence-to-activity deep networks now predict the regulatory output of short DNA
sequences with high accuracy, but optimizing nucleotide edits against a single such model
can exploit idiosyncrasies of that predictor rather than transferable regulatory signal.
This problem limits the practical utility of model-guided CRE design for downstream
experimental screens, where synthesis budget is limited and cross-model robustness is
essential. Here we present SafeEdit-CRE, a computational regulatory-sequence design
framework that selects cell-type-selective variants of natural 200-nt cis-regulatory
elements (CREs) under fixed substitution budgets of 1, 5, 10, or 20 nucleotides.
SafeEdit-CRE couples the published Malinois cell-type-specificity predictor with an
architecture-diverse independent evaluation ensemble, validation-calibrated
uncertainty, sequence-domain controls, and constrained beam search; candidates are
then passed to a second independently trained multi-kernel ensemble for
cross-model generalization assessment. Across 600 held-out natural CRE parents in K562,
HepG2, and SK-N-SH cells, SafeEdit-CRE increased cross-model cell-type-specificity margin
gain relative to iterative greedy search (0.877 vs.\ 0.822; paired difference 0.055;
95\% CI 0.040--0.071). Against a compute-matched beam search using only the primary
predictor, SafeEdit-CRE retained equivalent specificity gain (difference $-0.004$;
95\% CI $-0.011$--0.004) while lowering cross-model ensemble uncertainty (0.314 vs.\
0.335) and reducing aggressive target gain excursions. In a locked non-overlapping 96-parent
design-criteria benchmark, SafeEdit-CRE produced 61.5\% designs passing all prespecified
sequence and cross-model checks, compared with 38.5\% for greedy and 13.0\% for random
substitutions. We report 78 experimentally actionable Tier A candidate sequences across
all three cell types and four edit budgets, together with a tiered library suitable for
compact first-pass reporter screening. SafeEdit-CRE provides a general and reproducible
strategy for converting model-guided regulatory-sequence optimization into cross-model
candidate selection, yielding experimentally actionable CRE variants under realistic
editing budgets.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and code availability}
The source MPRA measurements are publicly available from Gosai et
al.~\citep{Gosai2024}. All code, environment files, frozen configuration manifests,
parent identifiers, candidate-level result tables, statistical summaries, analysis
scripts, fixed model seeds, and the 78 Tier A candidate sequences are archived in a
permanent Zenodo repository with an assigned DOI (\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.XXXXXXXX}; version v1.0). The repository additionally includes a minimal
end-to-end reproduction script that regenerates the main figures and statistical
tables from the released summary inputs. A public mirror of the source repository is
maintained at \url{https://github.com/Haangyeon/SafeEdit-CRE} (release tag v1.0),
where issue tracking and post-publication updates will be hosted. Pretrained model
checkpoints are included in the Zenodo archive.
