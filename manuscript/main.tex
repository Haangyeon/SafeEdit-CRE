\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background/Objectives:} Cis-regulatory elements (CREs) encode cell-type-selective
gene expression, yet sequence-to-activity models are rarely converted into compact,
experimentally actionable variant panels that preserve natural regulatory scaffolds.
We developed SafeEdit-CRE to select minimal edits that preserve cross-model
specificity while substantially reducing predictive uncertainty, producing auditable
CRE variant libraries ready for reporter screening.\\
\textbf{Methods:} SafeEdit-CRE combines the published Malinois cell-type-specificity
predictor with an architecture-diverse reviewer ensemble, validation-calibrated
uncertainty, sequence-domain controls, and constrained beam search. Natural 200-nt CREs
were edited under fixed budgets of 1, 5, 10, or 20 nucleotides and evaluated by a
separately trained multi-kernel ensemble fully excluded from candidate generation.\\
\textbf{Results:} Across 600 held-out CRE parents in K562, HepG2, and SK-N-SH cells,
cross-model specificity-margin gain increased from 0.822 for greedy editing to 0.877
for SafeEdit-CRE (paired difference 0.055; 95\% CI 0.040--0.071). Against a
compute-matched primary-model beam, SafeEdit-CRE closely matched predicted specificity
(difference $-0.004$; 95\% CI $-0.011$--$0.004$), while cross-model ensemble uncertainty
decreased from 0.335 to 0.314. In a locked confirmatory benchmark of 90 non-overlapping
parents, 60.6\% of SafeEdit-CRE designs passed all nine prespecified sequence and
model-agreement checks, compared with 38.8\% for greedy and 13.1\% for random
substitutions (paired difference +21.8 pp; 95\% CI 18.3--25.3).\\
\textbf{Conclusions:} SafeEdit-CRE provides a reproducible sequence-engineering
strategy for interrogating cell-type-specific regulatory grammar and yields a curated
library of 74 Tier A CRE variants prioritized for reporter screening across three cell
types and four edit budgets.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusions}
SafeEdit-CRE establishes cross-model uncertainty-aware minimal editing as a practical
route for converting sequence-to-activity models into compact, cell-type-selective CRE
variant libraries. By retaining natural sequence scaffolds and explicitly controlling
edit distance, model agreement, and predictive uncertainty, the framework provides a
focused starting point for functional-genomics studies of regulatory sequence grammar.

\section*{Data Availability Statement}
The processed MPRA measurements used to train Malinois are provided in Supplementary
Table 2 of Gosai et al.~\citep{Gosai2024}. The associated raw data, processing
notebooks, model weights, and immunofluorescence images are archived at Zenodo record
10698014. All SafeEdit-CRE code, environment files, frozen configuration manifests,
parent identifiers, candidate-level result tables, statistical summaries, analysis
scripts, fixed model seeds, and the 74 Tier A candidate sequences are archived in a
permanent Zenodo repository with an assigned DOI (\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21458489}; version v1.0.0). The repository additionally includes a minimal
end-to-end reproduction script that regenerates the main figures and statistical
tables from the released summary inputs. A public mirror of the source repository is
maintained at \url{https://github.com/Haangyeon/SafeEdit-CRE} (release tag v1.0.0),
where issue tracking and post-publication updates will be hosted. Pretrained model
checkpoints are included in the Zenodo archive.
