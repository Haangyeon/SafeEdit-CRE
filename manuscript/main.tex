\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background/Objectives:} MPRA and sequence-to-activity models have moved
cis-regulatory element (CRE) research from retrospective annotation toward predictive
design, and recent work has begun generating synthetic enhancers. Yet score-driven
optimization can produce model-specific or heavily rewritten sequences, leaving a
practical gap between prediction and compact experimental constructs. We developed
SafeEdit-CRE, a cross-model, uncertainty-aware minimal-editing framework that preserves
natural CRE scaffolds while producing auditable, experimentally actionable variant
panels.\\
\textbf{Methods:} SafeEdit-CRE combines the published Malinois cell-type-specificity
predictor with an architecture-diverse reviewer ensemble, validation-calibrated
uncertainty, sequence-domain safeguards, and constrained beam search. Natural 200-nt
CREs were edited under fixed budgets of 1, 5, 10, or 20 nucleotides; selected variants
were evaluated by a separately trained multi-kernel ensemble excluded from generation,
thereby separating search, review, and final evaluation.\\
\textbf{Results:} Across 600 held-out CRE parents in K562, HepG2, and SK-N-SH cells,
cross-model specificity-margin gain increased from 0.822 for greedy editing to 0.877
for SafeEdit-CRE (paired difference 0.055; 95\% CI 0.040--0.071). Against a
compute-matched primary-model beam, SafeEdit-CRE maintained closely matched predicted
specificity (difference $-0.004$; 95\% CI $-0.011$--$0.004$) while reducing ensemble
uncertainty from 0.335 to 0.314. In a locked benchmark of 90 non-overlapping parents,
60.6\% of designs passed all nine prespecified sequence and model-agreement checks,
compared with 38.8\% for greedy and 13.1\% for random substitutions (paired
difference +21.8 pp; 95\% CI 18.3--25.3).\\
\textbf{Conclusions:} SafeEdit-CRE delivers a curated Tier A library of 74
reporter-screening-ready variants spanning three cell types and four edit budgets.
By converting a large search space into a compact set of low-burden, cross-model
supported edits, it can reduce synthesis and screening load while retaining natural
sequence context. The framework therefore links regulatory-grammar analysis with a
direct engineering route to synthetic regulatory elements, functional noncoding-variant
prioritization, and cell-type-selective CRE design.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusions}
SafeEdit-CRE establishes cross-model uncertainty-aware minimal editing as a practical
route for converting sequence-to-activity models into compact, cell-type-selective CRE
variant libraries. By retaining natural sequence scaffolds and explicitly controlling
edit distance, model agreement, and predictive uncertainty, the framework provides a
focused starting point for functional-genomics studies of regulatory sequence grammar.

\section*{Data Availability Statement}
The processed MPRA measurements used to train Malinois are provided in Supplementary
Table 2 of Gosai et al.~\citep{Gosai2024}. The associated raw data, processing
notebooks, model weights, and immunofluorescence images are archived at Zenodo record
10698014. All SafeEdit-CRE code, environment files, frozen configuration manifests,
parent identifiers, candidate-level result tables, statistical summaries, analysis
scripts, fixed model seeds, and the 74 Tier A candidate sequences are assembled in
the upload-ready v1.1.0 release bundle. The bundle will be deposited in a permanent
Zenodo record and mirrored at \url{https://github.com/Haangyeon/SafeEdit-CRE} before
publication; the final DOI and commit hash will be inserted in the published version.
During peer review, the complete release bundle is available from the corresponding
author. It includes a minimal end-to-end reproduction script that regenerates the main
figures and statistical tables from the released summary inputs.
